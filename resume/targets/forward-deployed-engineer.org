* Summary

The request I keep receiving from other teams is not "build me a dashboard." It is "make this run
without me." So I do not build dashboards. I go to the person who owns the domain, learn the units
and periods and inclusion rules their numbers actually obey, and stand up an agent that works in
their language against their live systems — read-only where the evidence must stay honest, with a
trail back to the single record behind every figure. Two of those are in daily use at my company:
a VOC workbench the operations team reads every morning, and an incident workbench that aligns
platform catalog, device logs, and runtime state on one time axis and stops rather than warns when
its evidence contract breaks.

I can do this because I have built every layer underneath it — embedded firmware and protocols,
backend services, deployment, and the agent harnesses themselves. Connecting data to a model is
the part many people can do; the part that decides whether it survives contact with a real
organization is the domain owner's language, the boundary where the agent must stop, and the
hand-off that lets the next human pick it up. That is also why documentation and enablement are
not a side duty for me: I have spent years turning what I learned into skills, guides, and work
surfaces someone else can take over.

- @@latex:\cvlabel{Applied Agent Deployment}@@ — attaching agents to real operational data, evidence

- @@latex:\cvlabel{Legacy System Integration}@@ — putting an agent surface on systems already running:

- @@latex:\cvlabel{Full-Stack Delivery}@@ — firmware, backend, app, and infrastructure, so the
  integration is designed by someone who has had to make each layer work.
